Post-Training | OPD Beyond the Teacher: Understanding and Preserving Distillation Overshoot in Agentic On-Policy Distillation

Jike Zhong, Ming Li, Yuxiang Lai, Xi Xiao, Sheng Zhang, Ritwick Chaudhry, Tianle Chen, Jingyu Xie, Zheda Mai, Shao-Yuan Lo, Konstantinos Psounis, Yuanyuan Lei

arXiv preprint, 2026

We identify why student agents surpass their teachers during distillation, then lose those gains. Our success-preserving method, SPOD, improves final success by up to 15.2 percentage points over standard OPD and 20 points over the teacher.

Paper Code

Post-Training | RL | Shortcut Learning From Shortcuts to Reasoning: Robust Post-Training of Theory of Mind with Reinforcement Learning

Jike Zhong, Yuxiang Lai, Ming Li, Yuheng Li, Wuao Liu, Behzad Dariush, Konstantinos Psounis, Shao-Yuan Lo

ICML 2026

We expose shortcuts that inflate apparent theory-of-mind reasoning performance. On shortcut-resistant datasets, reinforcement fine-tuning with explicit reasoning improves generalization, higher-order reasoning, and robustness to counterfactuals.

Paper
↗

Agentic AI | Memory | Evaluation DSV-Mem: Evaluating Multimodal Memory in Professional Workflows for MLLM Agents

Jike Zhong, Ritwick Chaudhry, Xuanbai Chen, Tianchen Zhao, Linghan Xu, Yifan Xing, Nishant Sankaran

arXiv preprint, 2026

We benchmark how multimodal agents track and reconcile evolving, information-dense visual artifacts across professional workflows. DSV-Mem exposes state reconstruction as a key memory bottleneck and shows the value of explicit state tracking.

Paper
↗

Agentic AI | Thinking-With-Image | VLM Reasoning | Evaluation TIR-Bench: A Comprehensive Benchmark for Agentic Thinking-with-Images Reasoning

Ming Li*, Jike Zhong*, Shitian Zhao, Haoquan Zhang, Shaoheng Lin, Yuxiang Lai, Chen Wei, Konstantinos Psounis, Kaipeng Zhang

ECCV 2026

We evaluate agents that use tools and code to manipulate visual evidence across 13 tasks and 1,215 examples. Testing 22 multimodal models reveals substantial gaps in agentic visual reasoning; TIR-Bench also informs Qwen3.5 and Qwen3.6 evaluations.

Paper Code
↗

Post-Training | Multimodal RL | Adaptive Thinking To Think or Not To Think: A Study of Thinking in Rule-Based Visual Reinforcement Fine-Tuning

Ming Li, Jike Zhong, Shitian Zhao, Yuxiang Lai, Haoquan Zhang, Wang Bill Zhu, Kaipeng Zhang

NeurIPS 2025 · Spotlight

We show that explicit thinking is not always beneficial during visual reinforcement fine-tuning: direct-answer training can match or outperform it on perception tasks while reducing computation. We further explore how models learn whether to think based on their capabilities and task complexity.
